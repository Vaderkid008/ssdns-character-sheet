# DMCC + Bestiary design principles (Jessey, 2026-10-06)

The goal: the DM's eyes stay on the table, not the screen. Every click saved is attention given back to the players.

1. **Turn flow.** Clear turn order, one-click Next, and an on-deck heads-up for the next player.
2. **Everything about an enemy in one place.** AC, HP, named attacks (to-hit, damage, range), saves, DCs, traits, spells. No tab-flipping, no PDF.
3. **One-click resolution.** Roll vs the target's AC, show hit/miss, apply damage. Undo for misclicks.
4. **HP and conditions at a glance.** Bloodied/down, condition badges, auto-ticking durations.
5. **Trust beats features.** Never drop the DM, lose state, or double-apply. Stability first.
6. **DM controls visibility.** Private rolls, hidden AC/HP/DC, and the option to reveal a roll for drama.
7. **Fast improvising.** Free dice box, quick custom enemy, on-the-fly HP and condition edits.
8. **Tactics notes on every enemy.** One line, e.g. "flees at half HP" or "focuses the caster", so the DM plays enemies smart without memorizing them.

## Bestiary authoring checklist (each entry)
- Named attacks in attackList with toHit, damage, damageType, range, capacity/misfire for guns
- Riders as structured save/DC/condition/rounds
- Saves + skills structured (rollable), all six scores
- DCs spelled out in text the DM can read aloud
- Spells: mode (attack/save/heal/utility), one-line summary, uses/slots
- A tactics/morale trait
- Loot priced in ES; cover written as Take Cover only
